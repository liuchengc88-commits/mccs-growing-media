Cannabis Clone Propagation Plugs: What Buyers Should Verify

MCCS Growing Media · October 6, 2026

For licensed cannabis propagation facilities, a root photograph is a useful conversation starter, not a purchase specification. A molded propagation plug should be shortlisted against the actual tray, the facility's approved workflow and documented sample observations. This guide focuses on growing-media procurement, not on producing or supplying cannabis plants.

Separate an illustration from evidence

The lead image was generated with AI to explain the intended application. Its root density, leaf appearance and plug dimensions are not measured product data. MCCS has not documented a cannabis trial with this image, and no rooting timeline or survival percentage should be inferred from it.

The original exhibition photographs below are a separate record of rooted plant cuttings supplied by MCCS from its Yunnan exhibition participation. They have not been identified as cannabis. They show the plug outline and visible external roots at the time of photography; they do not establish cannabis suitability, sterility, faster rooting or a yield advantage.

Build a shortlist from the tray and material records

Start with the tray drawing or measured top opening, lower opening and cell depth. Include the tray brand and cell count, drainage arrangement, loading method and removal method. A cell count alone does not establish fit. Compare these details with the approved model catalog rather than estimating plug size from an AI image or a hand-held photograph.

Ask which material specification applies to the proposed model and batch. MCCS supplies molded coconut coir and peat substrate plugs; the exact proposed formulation and relevant documents must be confirmed for the project. Do not assume a composition, binder ratio, washing treatment, EC or pH value from color or appearance.

When reviewing a report, match the product identification, sample or batch reference, date and test method to the proposed supply. A report for another material or a literature result for another crop is not a model-specific cannabis qualification. Define any acceptance requirements with your facility team before testing.

Keep a sample record that a buyer can compare

Use your facility's approved propagation procedures and a current-media reference. Keep the starting count, crop or cultivar reference, plug model, batch and observation dates beside each group. Record differences in the conditions and handling instead of attributing every difference to the substrate.

Photograph representative samples from the same views, including the plug sides and base. Retain less successful specimens in the record as well as attractive ones. Record the count meeting your facility-defined rooted and established criteria, losses, plug breakage, loose material and removal difficulties.

Evaluate handling at the actual moisture state used in the facility. A photograph of a cohesive plug does not establish zero shedding during removal or transport. Record what happened during the same handling sequence for each group and continue the record after the facility's normal transplant step.

Compare cost per established plant, not plug price alone

A simple procurement estimate is: (plug purchase cost + allocated freight + handling labor cost) divided by the number of established plants. Use the same currency and the same definition of an established plant for both options. Include the observation period and starting count; do not compare a selected photo from one group with the whole batch from another.

Enter actual supplier quotations and your own observations. This estimate is not an MCCS quotation and does not predict rooting or survival. Keep taxes, import charges and other costs separate unless they are included consistently. If no plants meet the agreed criteria, report that outcome rather than calculating a cost with a zero denominator.

Prepare a specific request for a growing-media sample

Send the lawful commercial application, tray drawing or measurements, intended sample quantity, delivery country and the documents you need. Include the current medium and the workflow issue you want to assess, such as tray removal or rooted-plug handling. Those details are more useful than asking for the best cannabis plug without a defined requirement.

Confirm the sample model, availability, freight arrangement and required documents before dispatch. Do not infer fit, MOQ, carton quantity or price from the illustrations. MCCS coordinates growing-media supply; this guide does not offer cannabis cuttings, seeds or plant material.

Buyer questions

Are these cannabis rooting results?

No. The cannabis illustration is AI-generated. The original exhibition photos are rooted-cutting references without a verified cannabis crop identification.

Can a model be selected from the image?

No. Use the current product catalog and actual tray measurements, then confirm with project samples.

Are molded coir or peat plugs a guaranteed rockwool replacement?

No. The proposed format and material should be evaluated against the facility's current medium and approved workflow. This guide makes no comparative performance guarantee.

What should a technical report identify?

The tested product or material, sample or batch reference, test date and method. Confirm that its scope matches the proposed supply.

Does the trial record provide a cultivation recipe?

No. It records procurement observations. Licensed facilities should use their own approved procedures and applicable requirements.
