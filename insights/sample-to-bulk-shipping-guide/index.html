From Sample to Bulk: A Practical Shipping Guide

Sample-pack, trial-order and bulk-shipment planning for substrate plug sourcing.

June 4, 2026 · MCCS Growing Media

Many substrate plug projects begin with a small sample request, but the commercial goal is usually repeat supply. A clear shipping plan helps buyers move from first review to trial order and then to a stable bulk-order workflow.

Stage 1: samples

Samples are used to check size, material feel, hydration, tray fit and first impression. A typical sample pack may include 20–50 pieces per model, depending on size and application. Sample preparation is usually 3–5 working days after model selection and sample count are confirmed. Courier delivery commonly takes 5–9 working days, depending on the destination country, customs process and courier network.

Sample freight is normally paid by the buyer or shipped under the buyer’s courier account. If damage or model mismatch is confirmed with photos, replacement pieces can be discussed or added to the next shipment.

Stage 2: trial order

A trial order gives the buyer enough pieces to test real handling, germination, planting or retail packing. This stage is especially useful for private-label projects because packaging size, instruction card placement and product movement during transport can be checked before mass production.

Trial orders also help confirm carton quantity. The same product model may have different carton counts if the buyer chooses bulk packing, a retail box, a refill pouch or a complete starter kit. For this reason, carton quantity should be treated as a planning reference until packaging is confirmed.

Stage 3: bulk shipment

Bulk shipping should be planned after product and packaging are approved. Carton quantity is not fixed by model alone. It depends on outer carton size, plug size, retail packaging, private-label inserts and shipment method. Buyers should therefore treat carton quantity as a reference rather than a final promise.

The final shipping discussion should include destination market, required documents, carton markings and preferred delivery method. This creates a more predictable workflow for both parties and reduces the risk of last-minute changes during packing.
