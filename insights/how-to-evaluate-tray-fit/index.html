How to Evaluate Tray Fit and Plug Size

Tray opening, plug height and holder compatibility considerations for B2B sourcing.

June 4, 2026 · MCCS Growing Media

Tray fit is one of the most important details in substrate plug sourcing. Even when a plug looks suitable in a photo, a small mismatch in top diameter, bottom diameter or height can create handling problems during sowing, transplanting or retail kit assembly.

Every product in the MCCS catalog includes a tray-fit note to help buyers narrow their selection. These notes should be used as a starting point rather than a substitute for real sample testing. Tray suppliers, hydroponic system manufacturers and garden kit brands may all use slightly different holder dimensions.

Three measurements matter

The first measurement is the tray or holder opening. A plug with a wide top may sit well in one tray and too high in another. The second measurement is base diameter or bottom shape. A tapered plug needs enough depth to sit securely without falling through the cavity. The third measurement is height. If a plug is too tall, it may interfere with lids or kit packaging; if it is too short, it may not provide enough substrate volume for the intended stage.

The listed size on the product page is the wet, expanded size. This is the most relevant measurement because buyers use the plug after hydration or expansion. For dry storage and packing, carton quantity is estimated separately and may change by packaging method.

Model examples

CF-128 suits buyers working with 105-cell or 128-cell propagation trays. CF-072 is better aligned with 72-cell tray programs. CF-200 is positioned for high-density plant-factory style use where compact dimensions matter. CF-005 is useful where the cavity is square rather than round.

Orchid models such as CF-017, CF-025 and CF-035 should be evaluated by plant stage and target pot size rather than by tray count alone. Succulent products such as CF-067 or CF-901/902/903 are more dependent on container display and packaging protection than high-density tray use.

How to test fit properly

A reliable fit test should use the actual tray or holder that will be used in production or sold to end consumers. The buyer should test the plug after hydration, insert it into the tray, check stability, observe whether the top sits too high, and confirm whether the base leaves enough space for drainage or root movement.

For purchasing teams, the most efficient method is to request a sample set of two or three candidate models and test them in the exact tray or container. A simple fit test can prevent problems later in packing, transplanting and customer use.
